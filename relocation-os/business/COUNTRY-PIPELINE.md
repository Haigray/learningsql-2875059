# Which country to build next

## The scoring model

Rank candidates on five factors. The first three set the ceiling; the last two set the cost.

| Factor | Why it matters | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand** | US search volume and relocation intent. No demand, no business. | ×3 |
| **Complexity** | Number of genuinely distinct pathways and how hard the choice between them is. A country where the answer is "apply online, wait three weeks" cannot sustain a $30 guide. | ×3 |
| **Information gap** | How wrong or stale the free English-language material is. This is the wedge. | ×2 |
| **Verifiability** | Can a buyer phone or email an authority and get an answer, in English, on a public portal? The navigator edition depends entirely on this. | ×2 |
| **Churn** | How fast the rules move. **Moderate churn is an asset; extreme churn raises maintenance cost.** | ×1 |

### The counter-intuitive one

Rule changes look like a reason to avoid a country. For a maintained-guide business they are the opposite. A rule change simultaneously:

- invalidates every competing guide, blog post, and video overnight
- creates a spike of high-intent search from people who just discovered their plan is broken
- proves the thing your product claims — that it is maintained and everyone else's is not

Static PDFs die on churn. This product eats it. The only real limit is your own maintenance capacity, which is why churn is weighted ×1, not ×3 — it is a benefit with a cost attached, not a pure good.

## Why China fails the model

It scores worst on the factor the business model cannot survive: **verifiability**.

1. **The navigator edition breaks.** Your no-adviser strategy rests on a buyer being able to contact an authority and get an answer. Chinese primary sources are in Chinese, official guidance is thinly published in English, and there is no equivalent of Vietnam's e-visa portal or Cambodia's FWCMS that a buyer can independently check. Without verification steps, every claim reverts to "trust us" — which is exactly the position the product exists to avoid.
2. **The tree is thin.** Despite the K visa's launch in October 2025, employment still requires conversion to Z status and a work permit through an employer. Strip that out and there is little left to branch on — a decision tree with two real answers is not a $30 product.
3. **The K visa is not yet specified.** Duration per entry, validity period, and even the age limit were still unannounced. A pack would be mostly watchlist.
4. **The interest is curiosity, not intent.** Search volume spiked on news coverage comparing the K visa to H-1B changes. People reading about a policy story are not people buying a relocation guide.

Revisit if the K visa gets published implementing detail and an English application channel.

## On Thailand's "recent changes" specifically

The instinct that the 2026 changes make Thailand less attractive is worth testing, because the changes are larger than generally reported and they point the other way commercially:

- **From 31 August 2026**, DTV applicants must be a national or permanent resident of the country where they apply. This kills the single most widely-repeated piece of advice in the entire Thai nomad ecosystem — that you can apply from a neighbouring country. Every guide, forum post, and video recommending a run to Vientiane or Ho Chi Minh City is now wrong.
- A **criminal record check** is now required.
- The **฿500,000 seasoning requirement** is strictly enforced, and language schools have been excluded from the soft-power category.
- **Banking:** major Thai banks now treat the DTV as tourist-classified. Most holders cannot open accounts, and existing accounts are being frozen or closed. Almost nobody has written this up honestly.
- **Tax:** under Por. 161/2566 all remitted foreign income is taxable for tax residents, and the 2025 proposal for a two-year remittance grace period **still has not been enacted**. A large number of people are planning around relief that is not law.

That is five separate ways the standard advice is currently wrong, in the highest-demand Asian destination, all within one year. It is the best possible moment to enter with a maintained product, and the worst possible moment to publish a static PDF.

**Cost to be honest about:** Thailand will be roughly twice the maintenance load of Vietnam. Budget it deliberately, and put a `checkBy` date on every one of those five items the day the pack ships.

## Portfolio logic

Thailand is the country people compare Vietnam and Cambodia *against*. Holding all three makes the catalogue the mainland Southeast Asia authority rather than three unrelated guides, and it unlocks a comparison SKU — one decision tree, three countries, priced above any single guide — which is the natural upsell and the natural app feature.

Take Japan fourth, precisely because it breaks the pattern: different income bracket, different problem, stable rules, and it stops the catalogue from being read as "cheap Asia only."
